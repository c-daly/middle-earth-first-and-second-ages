# What the peoples of the two Ages could make

*Chapter 16 working notes for First and Second Age RM2 campaigns*

A ruler who owns a seeing-stone can send news across a kingdom. Whether her masons can build a tower for it, whether its keeper has authority to use it, and whether anyone can replace it if it is lost are three different questions. A settlement entry should tell the GM what can be made there, what must be brought in, and what works only because a particular maker, ally, or heirloom is present. Those distinctions set the price of a broken tool, the time it takes news to travel, and the choices available during a siege.

## Workshops and public works by Age

| People and period | Evidence of capability | What it means locally |
|---|---|---|
| Dwarves of Belegost and the Sindar of Doriath, First Age | The Dwarves help make Menegroth. The smiths of Belegost devise linked mail, and the Sindar learn smithcraft through their dealings with the Dwarves. Daeron’s runes travel in the other direction, carried east by Dwarves. [*The Silmarillion*, “Of the Sindar”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1217) | Date a Doriath encounter against that exchange. A Dwarf can be the source of a coat of mail, a Sinda its local repairer, and a pupil east of the mountains the reader of a Sindarin inscription. |
| Noldor of Beleriand and their captors, First Age | Gondolin has roads, gates, and skilled workers. One early tradition describes a portable blue crystal lamp whose secret of manufacture is lost even among the Noldor. Morgoth exploits captured Noldorin miners, among them Gwindor. [*Unfinished Tales*, “Of Tuor and His Coming to Gondolin,” note 2](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) · [Gwindor’s captivity](https://www.silmarillionwritersguild.org/node/4405) | An inherited light could let a surveyor inspect a confined shaft. Outfitting a whole mine with such lamps would require a surviving supply or a new discovery. Escaping workers may carry knowledge out of Angband. |
| Edain and Drúedain, First Age | Haldad’s people build a stockade between rivers. The Drúedain already have carving tools and plant pigments. As metal tools become easier to obtain through contact with Elves and Dwarves, their carvings become more ambitious. [Haldad’s stockade](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55752) · [*Unfinished Tales*, “The Drúedain”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) | A carver can know more about working stone than the merchant who sells her a chisel. Losing the trade route changes the jobs she can undertake and the time they take. |
| Eastern boatbuilders and the Entwives of Anduin, First Age into Second | The ancestors of Hador’s people develop boats capable of crossing a stormy inland sea. In a much earlier tradition the Teleri also improved their boats on the Sea of Rhûn. Treebeard remembers the Entwives’ gardens east of Anduin before and after Morgoth’s overthrow. [First Age accounts in the gazetteer](gazetteer-kingdoms-and-regions.md#first-age-events-east-of-the-blue-mountains) | Knowing how to cross open water or keep an orchard productive is a regional capability. A boatwright or gardener can be the person a royal expedition has to consult. |
| Eregion and Khazad-dûm, Second Age | Mining, metalwork, writing, and stonework meet at the West-gate. Narvi makes the doors and Celebrimbor draws their signs. The road serves sustained traffic between the two peoples before Sauron destroys Eregion. [*The Fellowship of the Ring*, “A Journey in the Dark”](https://www.encyclopedia-of-arda.com/d/doorsofdurin.php) · [*Unfinished Tales*, “The History of Galadriel and Celeborn”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) | The same gate can be easy to use in an age of friendship and hard to enter after the people who knew its word have gone. Its surviving workmanship can outlast the institution it served. |
| Númenor across the Second Age | Ocean navigation, shipbuilding, forestry, and harbour works develop together. Aldarion establishes Vinyalondë at the mouth of the Gwathló. Later royal archers are described with bows of hollow steel. The building of fleets draws mainland forest peoples into conflict. [*Unfinished Tales*, “A Description of the Island of Númenor,” “Aldarion and Erendis,” and “The Port of Lond Daer”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) | A royal arsenal, a shipyard at Rómenna, and a visiting captain’s carpenter have different resources in the same year. Felling rights upriver can decide whether a ship damaged at the haven sails again this season. |
| Arnor and Gondor, late Second Age | The Exiles bring seven palantíri from Númenor. Elendil and his sons distribute them among specific sites. Gondor itself has a river capital and fortified seats on either side of Anduin. [“Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1233/of-the-rings-of-power-and-the-third-age) | Two royal stations can exchange intelligence while a town between them still sends a rider. Possessing a palantír tells us who might receive news. It supplies no evidence that another stone can be ordered from a local workshop. |

**Rhûn, Harad, and Khand.** The gazetteer proposes river pilots, well keepers, assayers, orchard keepers, boatwrights, and winter guides. For those invented communities, describe a craft at the level of a job someone needs done. Who can read a channel after a flood? Who can repair a damaged terrace intake before the next irrigation turn? In the proposed Umbar campaign, local pilots know the approaches before Númenórean fortification. A later stone quay widens the choice of vessels that can unload there. Its builders still need the pilots to get them in. ## When magic changes what can be built

| Work | What builders, users, or besiegers can rely on | What remains to decide |
|---|---|---|
| **Tol-in-Gaurhoth, First Age. An explicitly bound structure.** Finrod built the tower on Tol Sirion. After Sauron occupies it, a spell binds its stones. Once he yields mastery of the tower, Lúthien undoes that binding and the prisoners’ pits lie open. [“Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226) | Possession of the stronghold gives Lúthien a way to open the whole prison. A mason or miner could still tell a rescuer where the cells had been cut. | Which parts of Finrod’s original construction did Sauron alter? Could an observer discover the binding while he still held the tower? |
| **The Doors of Durin, Second Age. An enchanted entrance.** The signs are traced in ithildin, which reflects moonlight and starlight under the conditions Gandalf describes. The spoken word opens the doors. [“A Journey in the Dark”](https://www.encyclopedia-of-arda.com/d/doorsofdurin.php) | A porter can admit a trading party without handling a key or knowing how to make either the doors or their inscription. The open approach makes sense during the years of trade. | Who else could fit such an entrance? Would the two makers undertake a second commission, and what would the patron have to supply? |
| **Barad-dûr, Second Age. Foundations dependent on the One.** The tower is begun centuries before its completion. Its foundations are made with the power of the Ring. The Last Alliance can raze the tower while its foundations endure. [Appendix B, “The Second Age”](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp) · [“The Council of Elrond,” on the foundations](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Barad-dur) | Victory gives the victors a ruin whose foundations can serve an enemy again. A Dúnadan surveyor has something consequential to report after the siege. | What signs could a surveyor recognize? How much of the explanation would Elrond or Isildur share with the people charged with watching the site? |
| **Orthanc and the outer wall of Minas Tirith.** Gandalf credits the Men of Númenor with building Orthanc. Its four black piers appear to form a single shaft until they part near the summit. At Minas Tirith the outward face of the outer wall resembles Orthanc and dates to the years when Númenórean craft still flourished in exile. [“The Council of Elrond”](https://esl-bits.eu/ESL.English.Learning.Audiobooks/1%20LOTR/14/text.html) · [“The Road to Isengard”](https://litarchive.online/the-two-towers) · [“The Siege of Gondor”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/the-return-of-the-king-j-r-r-tolkien/) | The Ents tear apart the surrounding works but cannot gain a grip on Orthanc or crack it. Minas Tirith’s outer face withstands steel and fire. The Gate remains vulnerable. [“Flotsam and Jetsam”](https://esl-bits.eu/ESL.English.Learning.Audiobooks/2%20LOTR/09/text.html) · [“The Siege of Gondor”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/the-return-of-the-king-j-r-r-tolkien/) | Pippin wonders whether an older wizardry endures in Orthanc. How the builders worked the stone, and whether it already possessed its extraordinary qualities, remain open. |

**The Stone of Erech.** A great black globe lies half buried on the hill. The keepers of the lore of Westernesse say that Isildur brought it from Númenor. Here the King of the Mountains swore allegiance to him. Whether the globe is of the same stone as Orthanc remains unknown. [“The Passing of the Grey Company”](https://bookwise.io/j-r-r-tolkien/the-lord-of-the-rings-volume-3) · [Chapter 8 on the oath at Erech](chapter-08-kinds-of-magic.md#oaths-curses-and-the-paths-of-the-dead)

**Galvorn and the black blades of the First Age.** Eöl learned metalwork among the Dwarves and devised galvorn, a shining black metal he could work thin enough to wear while it still turned blades and darts. He also forged Anglachel and Anguirel from the iron of a fallen star. The published *Silmarillion* leaves the relationship between the sword metal and galvorn open. It gives no link between galvorn and Númenórean masonry. [“Of Maeglin”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1223/of-maeglin) · [“Of Túrin Turambar”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1228)

Angrist, the knife Beren used to cut a Silmaril from Morgoth’s crown, was Telchar’s work. In an earlier telling the blade gleams pale, and the smiths of Nogrod sing enchantments as they forge its steel. MERP gives the weapons its own materials in *Treasures of Middle-earth*. Its entries assign galvorn to Eöl’s two swords and black eog to Angrist. [“Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226) · [*The Lay of Leithian*, lines 3054–3060](https://www.silmarillionwritersguild.org/node/5499) · [ICE’s weapon entries](https://pdfcoffee.com/treasures-of-middle-earth-pdf-free.html)

**A possible Dwarven building art.** Imagine a vault being driven from two faces in Khazad-dûm. A master and an apprentice sing a measured phrase at the end of each shift. In one version, its returning sound lets trained miners hear a concealed crack. Good listeners can learn the practice, the vault still needs shoring, and the apprentice may finish the job if the master goes away. In a more overt version, the master can lay a virtue in the finished arch that holds a fractured course together. Then builders can attempt a span they would otherwise abandon, and their patrons will want to know how long that virtue lasts. These are two campaign interpretations of forge-song and craft. Choose by their consequences for actual mines and halls. [Chapter 8 on the enchanter](chapter-08-kinds-of-magic.md#a-test-case-for-the-enchanter)

**Using RM2.** For a repair, first establish what failed. A washed-out approach to a word-gate calls for surveying, hauling, and ordinary road work. Damage to the signs or the mechanism raises a different question about who still knows the maker’s art. Use the relevant trained maneuver when the outcome is in doubt. Record how many workers can attempt it and what the settlement loses while the work is underway. That gives a player a reason to seek a particular teacher or ally years before a fortress comes under siege.
