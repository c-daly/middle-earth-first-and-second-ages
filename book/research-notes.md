# Research notebook

These entries concern places, periods, and rulings for which the book has yet to adopt a history. When a choice is adopted, record the source and reason in the book’s register and revise the affected chapter.

## Mordor before Sauron

**Evidence.** A late authorial note attributes Orodruin and its eruptions to the devastation of Melkor in the long First Age. It entertains an early Elvish use of the name Mordor. Neither an eruption date nor a particular project Morgoth conducted there is recorded. Shelob came to the western mountains before Sauron settled there and before construction of Barad-dûr. Tolkien gives no itinerary or year for her flight. The Gondorian tradition about the Drúedain places human migration south of the later Mordor, then in Ithilien and across Anduin. It does not identify the population within the basin. [The Peoples of Middle-earth, “Last Writings,” note 14, excerpted in Other Minds no. 1, p. 13](https://www2.omzine.org/downloads/om-english/om-001-Other_Minds_Magazine_Issue_001_revision_20080203.pdf) · [The Two Towers, “Shelob’s Lair”](https://bookwise.io/j-r-r-tolkien/the-lord-of-the-rings-volume-2) · [Unfinished Tales, “The Drúedain”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

**Limits on reconstruction.** Appendix B has Sauron begin his stronghold about S.A. 1000. The fires he used at Orodruin help explain his choice. The agricultural estates supplying his army from Núrnen are attested millennia later. Their existence does not date settlement of the lake basin or tell us who held the land before him. [The Silmarillion, “Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1233) · [The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp)

**Deferred.** Population, languages, routes within the basin, an eruption calendar, and any First Age outpost of Morgoth. A missing traveler at the western pass fits the evidence without deciding who farmed Núrnen. A campaign about conquest around the lake would need those people and their institutions established first.

## First Age Eriador

**Evidence.** Some of Amlach’s folk returned from Estolad into Eriador. Accounts of the Nandor also place Elven wanderers east of the Blue Mountains. Later traditions about the first Númenórean voyages record encounters with Men. Their dates matter. [The Silmarillion, “Of the Coming of Men into the West”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1224) · [Unfinished Tales, “Aldarion and Erendis”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

**Deferred.** Permanent towns, political boundaries, population density, and the destinations of particular returnees. A known migration supports occupied country and long stretches of wilderness. It gives no warrant for a continuous chain of market towns.

## Avarin settlements in the East

The Vale of Anduin has a surviving local name, *penni*. Tolkien gives five other Avarin forms of *Quendi* without comparable directions for mapping their homelands. An independent Avarin settlement in Rhûn in either Age remains a campaign possibility. Its site, language, and dealings with local Men still need a history. [Chapter 15 on the Avari and the Wood-elves](middle-earth-in-the-first-and-second-ages.md#the-peoples-of-middle-earth)

**A possibility for later.** Some Avari remained at Cuiviénen into the time of the first sunrise. A family might have stayed in that part of the ancient East for generations while the water withdrew, eventually making a life in dry country. An elder might remember fishing from a ledge now high above a well. A desert in the later basin is a campaign idea. Tolkien locates the old bay on the Sea of Helcar and leaves its position on later maps uncertain. [*The Silmarillion*, “Of the Sun and Moon”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1218) · [“Of the Coming of the Elves”](https://images.pcmac.org/SiSFiles/Schools/AL/HooverCity/SpainParkHigh/Uploads/DocumentsCategories/Documents/The%20Coming%20of%20Elves%20and%20captivity%20of%20Melkor.pdf)

## Oropher’s journey

**Evidence.** “The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves” places an established Silvan people in Greenwood. Oropher and his few Sindarin companions adopted their speech and naming customs. Eriador, west of the Misty Mountains, and the destination in Greenwood, east of them, require separate histories. [Unfinished Tales, Part Two, IV, Appendix B](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

**Possible scene.** At an Anduin crossing a Silvan ferrymaster knows which channel remains open in winter and which households owe a boat. Oropher arrives as a guest. This is an original encounter hook. The exact route, the names of his hosts, and how local households conferred or recognized his kingship remain undecided.

## Other matters held for consideration

- **The Vales of Anduin.** The prologue to *The Fellowship of the Ring* preserves Hobbit traditions about the upper vales. It does not supply a secure Second Age settlement. A river household is an optional campaign placement.
- **Entwives and Bombadil.** Use Treebeard’s account of the gardens and Letter 144. A later sighting of an Entwife or an explanation for Bombadil’s nature would require a deliberate departure. Local witnesses can remember encounters without resolving those mysteries.
- **Stature and incarnation.** Compare Glorfindel at the Ford, Fingolfin before Angband, and Huan with Sauron. The events support different kinds of spiritual presence and bodily injury. No numerical law of decline by Elven generation or universal law of healing for every Vala has been adopted.
- **Great masonry and mithril.** Distinguish the black globe at Erech, the piers of Orthanc, and the outer face of Minas Tirith. No common material, quarry, or treatment has been adopted. The discovery of mithril belongs in the history of Khazad-dûm. The precise circumstances of the first find are still to be established.
- **Later rules development.** The original design uses Rolemaster Second Edition as a mechanical reference and MERP as a setting resource. Any conversion should identify the actual procedure it changes and the consequence at the table. Professions, development costs, levelled spell lists, fear, and criticals remain separate working subjects.

- **The vault-song.** [Chapter 23](middle-earth-in-the-first-and-second-ages.md#23-what-the-peoples-of-the-two-ages-could-make) uses a trained ear to locate a shifting course. The stronger variant, in which a singer lays a lasting strength in the arch itself, remains a possible development. A decision would change which spans a Dwarven community can maintain after a particular master dies.
- **Early creatures and coercion.** Third Age winged mounts and the wights of Tyrn Gorthad have specific histories. Earlier wild populations or occupied tombs elsewhere need independent placement. [Freedom under a master](middle-earth-in-the-first-and-second-ages.md#freedom-under-a-master) sets out the rulings on Orcish spirit and free will reserved to the GM. That delegation is settled and is recorded in the book’s choice register.
