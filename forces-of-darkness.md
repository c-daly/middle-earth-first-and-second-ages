# The forces of darkness

*Chapter 15 working notes for First and Second Age RM2 campaigns*

At Sauron’s tower a wounded wolf escapes Huan, reaches his master, and lives long enough to tell him who is on the bridge. Draugluin’s last words change what Sauron does next. An encounter with one of the Enemy’s servants can turn on what it knows, whom it answers to, and whether it gets home. [*The Silmarillion*, “Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226)

## Orcs and their captains

First Age Orcs serve in Morgoth’s armies, hunt fugitives, and obey such orders as taking Húrin alive. In a Second Age note, eastern Orcs initially scorn Sauron in his fair form. A captain's authority therefore depends on the place, the year, and the people who will enforce an order. The GM determines the nature of Orcish spirits and the reach of a master’s power over their wills. Give a patrol a reason for being on the road and its leader something to lose by carrying out the order badly. An Orc told to bring a traveler back alive presents a different danger from a raider trying to get his companions home before daylight. [“Of the Fifth Battle”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1227) · [Chapter 14 on Orcs and free will](sourcebook-outline.md#where-did-orcs-come-from-how-free-are-they)

## Trolls and giants

**Trolls.** Gothmog has a troll-guard in the First Age. Húrin’s stand against it buys time for Turgon’s retreat. That is a model for a solo encounter with overwhelming opposition. The player’s question may be how many people reach the pass while it is held. Sunlight petrifies the three trolls in *The Hobbit*. What a Beleriand scout knows about a particular guard’s daylight habits is worth establishing before the encounter. The Olog-hai able to endure sunlight under Sauron’s will appear late in the Third Age. A Second Age troll garrison is a campaign placement, and its daylight arrangements are a useful clue for a scout. [“Of the Fifth Battle”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1227) · [*The Hobbit*, “Roast Mutton”](https://ae-lib.org.ua/texts-c/tolkien__the_hobbit__en.htm) · [*The Lord of the Rings*, Appendix F, on trolls](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Olog-hai)

**Giants.** The stone-giants of *The Hobbit* throw rocks across a mountain valley in a storm. Gandalf later considers asking a giant for help closing a passage. What threatens the travelers is the giants’ game. A quarrel with the Dark Lord enters neither scene. For either earlier Age, a giant who knows an old mountain road is a suggested inhabitant. A player could seek one out after the road collapses. Who knows how to attract its attention safely, and what favour would it remember? [*The Hobbit*, “Over Hill and Under Hill” and “Out of the Frying-Pan into the Fire”](https://ae-lib.org.ua/texts-c/tolkien__the_hobbit__en.htm)

## Drakes, dragons, and Balrogs

**Dragons.** *Drake* is a word for a dragon. Record separately whether a particular dragon flies, breathes fire, or can overpower a listener through its gaze or speech. Glaurung is a wingless fire-drake. At Nargothrond he holds Túrin in his gaze while the captives are taken away. At the end of the Age, Morgoth’s first assault with winged dragons comes as a terrible surprise. Ancalagon belongs to that moment. Later histories distinguish cold-drakes. A dragon surviving in a remote Second Age valley is plausible campaign material. Its range, gifts, and loyalties are decisions for the GM. [“Of Túrin Turambar”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1228) · [“Of the Voyage of Eärendil and the War of Wrath”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1231/of-the-voyage-of-e%C3%A4rendil-and-the-war-of-wrath) · [Appendix A, “Durin’s Folk,” on cold-drakes](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Cold-drakes)

**Balrogs.** These are Maiar who adhered to Morgoth. Gothmog commands in the field. After the War of Wrath the published account has a few survivors flee underground. Durin’s Bane is a survivor whose awakening in Khazad-dûm belongs to the Third Age. For a Second Age expedition a promising question is who believes a shaft ought to be sealed, what they actually saw below it, and what proof the mine’s owners would accept. A GM who wants the danger to remain a rumour can settle the miners’ testimony without settling what sleeps beneath them. [“Valaquenta”](https://edspace.american.edu/justinjacobs/wp-content/uploads/sites/986/2025/01/Silmarillion.pdf) · [The end of the Balrogs of Angband](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/56221) · [*The Lord of the Rings*, Appendix B](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp)

## Werewolves and vampires

Tolkien gives Sauron’s **werewolves** an unusually specific origin in the tale of Beren. Sauron has imprisoned dreadful spirits in the bodies of beasts. Draugluin speaks and reports to him. Sauron can also assume wolf-form himself, and Beren passes for a werewolf wearing Draugluin’s hide through Lúthien’s art. Those three wolves could look alike to a sentry. What a loremaster could learn about one by observing the others is a good question for play. A bite that transmits a curse or a change governed by the moon would be additions to this account. [“Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226)

**Thuringwethil** carries messages for Sauron and flies to Angband in vampire form. Tolkien describes clawed joints on her wings. Lúthien later wears her bat-fell, and the wounded Sauron himself takes vampire form to escape Huan. These passages establish a shape, its use, and a named messenger. Her underlying nature and the existence of a wider vampire people remain open. They give us no general rules for contagious bites, revenants, or daylight. For the First Age, a courier arriving by night can change an entire garrison’s orders. Any surviving Second Age messenger of her kind would be a campaign invention. [“Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226)

## The winged beasts of the Nazgûl

At the Pelennor the narrator wonders whether the Witch-king’s mount descends from a kind that survived in remote mountains from an older world. Sauron took and raised the individual beast and gave it to his servant. Its ancestry is presented as a possibility. Its service is documented in the Third Age. **An undiscovered wild population is a plausible option for either earlier Age. Winged mounts for Second Age Ringwraiths require a further choice.** A shepherd who has seen a winged scavenger return to an inaccessible ledge can give a player something to investigate long before anyone has thought of riding one. [*The Return of the King*, “The Battle of the Pelennor Fields”](https://esl-bits.eu/ESL.English.Learning.Audiobooks/3%20LOTR/06/text.html)

## A turn at the table

Consider an invented First Age encounter at a ferry below Tol-in-Gaurhoth. A wounded wolf reaches the bank ahead of the player. It calls the ferryman by his childhood name and orders him to untie the boat. A missing scout is still on the far bank. The first round gives the player a real choice between reaching the rope, answering the wolf, and slipping away to warn the scout. Use the ordinary RM2 maneuver or attack appropriate to the declared action. A successful listening or perception roll might pick up the scout’s answering whistle. Everyone close enough can hear the wolf speak. The wolf knows who the ferryman is and wants to cross. How it learned his name is a clue the GM can place elsewhere. Its allegiance, its ability to speak, and its physical danger each matter at a different point in the encounter.
