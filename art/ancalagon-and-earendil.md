## Tolkien’s account

In chapter 24 of *The Silmarillion*, Morgoth’s winged dragons drive back the host of the Valar amid thunder, lightning and fire. Eärendil arrives in white radiance aboard Vingilot, accompanied by the great birds under Thorondor. The aerial battle lasts through the day and the following night. Eärendil slays Ancalagon before sunrise, and the towers of Thangorodrim break beneath his fall. Tolkien calls him “the mightiest of the dragon-host.” His wingspan and length are unspecified. Earlier in the chapter, Vingilot has white timbers, silver sails and golden oars, and Eärendil wears the Silmaril on his brow. [“Of the Voyage of Eärendil and the War of Wrath”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1231)

There is one measured wingspan in the company. Tolkien gives Thorondor thirty fathoms, about 180 feet or 55 metres. He gives no ratio between eagle and dragon. Thangorodrim supplies the smoke in the painting. Morgoth raised those heights from furnace waste and excavated rock above Angband, and smoke rose from their summits. [“Of the Return of the Noldor”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55624) · [“Of Beleriand and Its Realms”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55645)

## The painting

Vingilot and the eagles are on the near side of a gap in the clouds. Ancalagon fills the farther sky from wingtip to tail. His proportions, the angle of approach and the narrow break in the eastern cloud are artistic choices. The glimmer on the horizon is the first light ahead of sunrise. Both PNGs are upscaled from the generated studies. The 16:9 composition extends the cloud above and below the confrontation to keep both wingtips in frame.
