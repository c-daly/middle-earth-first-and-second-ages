# The chaining of Melkor

[First cover study](melkor-before-manwe-cover-study-01.png) · [Second cover study](melkor-before-manwe-cover-study-02.png) · [The Halls of Mandos](melkor-before-manwe-cover-study-03-mandos.png) · [The council in separate seats](melkor-before-manwe-cover-study-04-mandos.png)

The scene is Melkor’s first judgment in the Ring of Doom, after the Valar make war for the sake of the newly awakened Elves. Tulkas wrestles him down at Utumno. When Melkor is brought before Manwë he is blindfolded, bound hand and foot, and lies face down. Tulkas holding the chain at the hearing and the arrangement of the witnesses are choices for this painting. The Iron Crown and the Silmarils belong to a later captivity. ## Third study · The Halls of Mandos

For the wide cover, the council sits inside Mandos. In the published account the Valar hear Melkor’s plea in the Ring of Doom and send him to Mandos afterward. The painting draws the two scenes together. Tolkien describes Vairë weaving the deeds of time into hangings that cover these halls. Here the subjects she has woven are the Lamps, their fall and the Two Trees. Nienna mourns the hurts Melkor has done to the world. Her tears at this gathering and Tulkas’s foot on the captive’s back are choices for the painting.
