# Lúthien in Angband

The second study follows the author's direction to ground Morgoth's appearance in Tolkien's description at this point in the story. The charcoal and gouache treatment is an exploratory choice. The old wound on his face is Thorondor's. Its precise shape and placement are artistic choices. His hands were burned black by the Silmarils. In the throne room all three jewels blaze white when the fires go out. ## Revised study

[Revised illustration](luthien-in-angband-study-02.png)

**Direction for the next pass.** Keep this study for now. Morgoth still looks too handsome. Give Thorondor’s old wound enough depth and asymmetry to disfigure the face visibly at the size of the printed illustration. The shape of the injury is ours to interpret. Retain the terrible majesty Tolkien gives him and the lasting burns on his hands.

Set all three Silmarils into the iron of the crown. Show heavy iron claws curling over each jewel’s rim, silhouetted against its white light. The grip should be visible at page size. Beren uses Angrist to cut one Silmaril free of just such a setting. [“Of Beren and Lúthien”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1226)

Built-in ImageGen edit prompt

> Precise edit of the supplied book illustration. Depict Morgoth as Tolkien describes him at the time of Lúthien's song, after the duel with Fingolfin. Change Morgoth's visible face, hands, and crown. The Silmarils have burned both hands permanently black. Render the bare skin down to each fingertip visibly coal-black and burn-scarred, readable in the sidelight, subtly stiff with lasting pain. Thorondor marred his face. Give the side of his face turned to the reader a single unmistakable deep old eagle-beak scar pulling the cheek and brow out of symmetry. Let the scar catch the light. His features are daunting, once majestic, austere and terrible in repose, his bowed head visibly burdened by a heavy, roughly forged iron crown. The three Silmarils, exactly three, blaze with living white flame against the dark iron. The look in his near-closed eyes should convey the menace of a sovereign who let Lúthien sing for his own dark purposes. Keep Lúthien, her dark embroidered cloak, the courtiers asleep, staging, painterly charcoal-and-gouache technique, original composition and landscape framing. Only the specific bodily marks Tolkien attests, no fresh gore, invented horns, fangs, fiery eyes or full-face helm. No text.
