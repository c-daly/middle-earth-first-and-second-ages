# Kingdoms and regions of the First and Second Ages

*Working gazetteer for Chapters 2 and 3.* Each entry gives the political or geographic name first. Cities, fortresses, rivers, and roads appear under the land they help a player find. A name in the first column can describe a homeland shared by several peoples.

## First Age

**Beleriand and its northern marches** form the main western theatre. The Sirion divides West and East Beleriand. The Blue Mountains mark the eastern edge of the map. Beyond them, Middle-earth continues. The northern front changes irreversibly when Morgoth breaks the Siege of Angband. [*The Silmarillion*, “Of Beleriand and Its Realms”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1221/of-beleriand-and-its-realms)

| Kingdom or region | Who lives or holds power there | Places and changes that matter in play |
|---|---|---|
| Angband and the northern plain | Morgoth rules from Angband beneath Thangorodrim. The Noldor hold the approaches during the Siege. | Before the Battle of Sudden Flame the grassy plain is Ard-galen. Afterwards it is the ash-covered Anfauglith. An old traveler's directions can describe a country that has ceased to exist. |
| Hithlum | Fingolfin and later Fingon rule here. Mithrim is also home to Sindar. Dor-lómin becomes the homeland of the House of Hador. | Lake Mithrim and the passes of the Ered Wethrin. After the Battle of Unnumbered Tears, surviving Hadorian families live under Easterling occupation. |
| Nevrast and Gondolin | Turgon’s people dwell first in coastal Nevrast, then in the hidden kingdom of Gondolin. | Vinyamar is the earlier seat. Gondolin lies inland in Tumladen, within the Encircling Mountains. Tuor encounters the abandoned halls before he ever sees the living city. |
| Doriath | Thingol and Melian rule a Sindarin kingdom centred on the forests of Neldoreth and Region. | Menegroth on the Esgalduin. Melian’s Girdle governs access for much of the Age. After Thingol's death and Melian's departure, its old reputation for safety becomes dangerous advice. |
| Nargothrond and the Narog valley | Finrod establishes the realm. Orodreth succeeds him. Its woods and outlying guard posts extend far beyond the underground city. | The Narog, the concealed approach to the halls, and the bridge built late in their history. How a stranger can approach depends on who has the king’s ear. |
| Dorthonion | Aegnor and Angrod hold the northern highlands. People of the House of Bëor settle in Ladros. | After the Battle of Sudden Flame, the highlands fall. In the forest later called Taur-nu-Fuin, Barahir and his companions survive for a time as outlaws. |
| Brethil | The Haladin, the People of Haleth, keep their own leaders in woodland occupied with Thingol’s leave. | The Crossings of Teiglin bring traffic to the forest's edge. A traveler seeking passage has good reason to speak to the people who watch the ford. |
| The Pass of Sirion | A strategic frontier between Hithlum, Dorthonion, and the interior of Beleriand. | Finrod builds Minas Tirith on Tol Sirion. After Sauron takes it, the island becomes Tol-in-Gaurhoth. A fortress built to protect the road can now close it. |
| The eastern marches | The sons of Fëanor hold several distinct territories. Maedhros holds Himring, Maglor guards the Gap, Celegorm and Curufin hold Himlad, and Caranthir holds Thargelion. | The Pass of Aglon, the open approaches through Maglor’s Gap, and Lake Helevorn. The siege breaking affects each lord differently. Bór’s and Ulfang’s peoples enter these lands with loyalties of their own. |
| Ossiriand | The Nandorin Green-elves inhabit the Land of Seven Rivers between the Gelion and the Blue Mountains. | River crossings and wooded valleys lead toward the Dwarf-roads. After Denethor’s death the Green-elves take no other king. A visitor needs to know which local people to approach. |
| The Blue Mountains | Belegost and Nogrod are separate Dwarven cities in the Ered Luin. | Mount Dolmed and the roads west into Beleriand. Knowing a smith in Belegost gives a character a particular connection. It confers little standing in Nogrod. |
| The Falas, Balar, and the Mouths of Sirion | Círdan’s Sindarin mariners, the Falathrim, live at the coastal havens of Brithombar and Eglarest. Balar and the Havens of Sirion become refuges as the western kingdoms fall. | In a late campaign, survivors of Gondolin and Doriath can meet at Sirion. The sea route to Balar matters once the northern roads and the ports of the Falas are lost. |

The territorial survey describes Beleriand near the height of Elven power. Read later journeys against the chapters on the Edain, the ruin of Beleriand, Túrin, and the falls of Doriath and Gondolin. Tolkien describes the two Dwarf-cities separately in “Of the Sindar.” [*The Silmarillion*](https://ae-lib.org.ua/texts-c/tolkien__the_silmarillion__en.htm) · [Belegost and Nogrod](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55569)

**Beyond Beleriand.** Aman lies across the Great Sea. For a campaign beginning before the Exile, distinguish Tirion in Eldamar, the Telerin haven of Alqualondë, and Valinor beyond the Pelóri. The crossing of the Helcaraxë belongs on Galadriel’s route with the exiles. East of the Blue Mountains, Khazad-dûm already exists in the Misty Mountains. Its history spans both Ages. [“Of the Flight of the Noldor”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1216/of-the-flight-of-the-noldor) · [*The Lord of the Rings*, Appendix B](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp)

### First Age events east of the Blue Mountains

The eastern sources preserve quite different kinds of memory. The Teleri’s Great Journey predates the rising of the Sun. The travels of the ancestors of Bëor and Hador belong to the later centuries in which Men enter the histories of Beleriand. Both give us events on the inland sea itself.

| Where | What Tolkien records |
|---|---|
| **Sea of Rhûn, during the Great Journey** | In a late note on Círdan, the Teleri spend a long time beside this lake. They had already made rafts and light paddle boats. Here they learn to build larger, stronger vessels, long before their people become famous as shipwrights on the western sea. [*The Peoples of Middle-earth*, “Last Writings,” note on Círdan](https://www.silmarillionwritersguild.org/node/4363) |
| **The inland sea, during the human migrations** | In “The Problem of Ros,” the ancestors of Hador’s people settle along a wooded northeastern shore. Once they develop their boatbuilding, they discover that relatives from whom they had long been separated live against the hills on the southwestern shore. These are the ancestors of Bëor’s people. The crossing is about two hundred miles, storms are fierce, and the visits are infrequent. Christopher Tolkien identifies the unnamed lake with the Sea of Rhûn. [*The Peoples of Middle-earth*, “The Problem of Ros,” including the editor’s note](https://greenbookofthewhitedowns.blogspot.com/2023/07/migrations-and-monuments-part-6.html) |
| **Hildórien and the East** | In the published *Silmarillion*, Men awaken in Hildórien at the first sunrise. An Elvish tradition says that Morgoth heard of their awakening, left Sauron in command, and went east himself. The later meeting with Finrod preserves a human perspective. Bëor remembers a darkness his people fled and declines to tell its story. His people had also lived alongside Elves east of the mountains long enough to learn much of their speech. [“Of the Coming of Men into the West”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1224) |
| **The Anduin and Eriador** | Some of the Nandor remain in the Anduin woods, some reach the river mouths, and others travel past the White Mountains into Eriador. When danger grows, Denethor gathers as many of his scattered people as he can and leads them west to seek safety with Thingol. Dwarves also carry Daeron’s runes east across the Blue Mountains, where other peoples come to know them. [The wanderings of the Nandor](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55579) · [The travels of the Cirth](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/paragraph/55580) |
| **The gardens east of Anduin** | Treebeard remembers the Entwives crossing the river and planting new gardens while the Darkness was in the North. He remembers their fields flourishing after its overthrow. His account gives a First Age campaign an Entwife clearing ground for an orchard that could still be there in the Second. [*The Two Towers*, “Treebeard”](https://esl-bits.eu/ESL.English.Learning.Audiobooks/2%20LOTR/04/text.html) |
| **South of the later Mordor, Ithilien, and the White Mountains** | A tradition attributed to Gondorian historians traces the Drúedain from the south. They turned north before reaching the coast of Harad, crossed Anduin, and settled in and below the White Mountains. Even the proposed crossing near the later Cair Andros is an inference within the account. This supplies a migration route. The settlements and dynasties of First Age Far Harad are open to invention. [*Unfinished Tales*, “The Drúedain,” notes](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) |

**An encounter on the Sea of Rhûn.** Start a player in one of the first eastern boats to reach the southwestern shore. The people who draw it up the beach have unfamiliar faces. Then one of them calls to a child using a word the pilot has only heard from her grandmother. Tolkien supplies the separation and the rediscovery. Who speaks first, what they recognize, and whether anyone is willing to make the return crossing belong to the campaign.

**Eriador before the drowning.** During the Long Peace, some of Amlach’s people chose a new leader and crossed back east over the Blue Mountains. The western annals lose track of them there. A traveler can therefore meet Men in First Age Eriador well before refugees from Beleriand arrive. The record gives no settlement names or population figures for these returnees. [The Silmarillion, “Of the Coming of Men into the West”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1224)

**The land later called Mordor.** A late note traces Orodruin and its eruptions to Melkor’s devastation during the long First Age. It gives neither the date the mountain rose nor a stronghold or forge that Morgoth kept there. In a Gondorian account, the Drúedain traveled from lands south of this region, turned north into Ithilien, and crossed Anduin. That tradition locates people on the southern and western approaches. The inhabitants of the basin around Núrnen in this period remain unnamed. [The Peoples of Middle-earth, “Last Writings,” note 14, excerpted in Other Minds](https://www2.omzine.org/downloads/om-english/om-001-Other_Minds_Magazine_Issue_001_revision_20080203.pdf) · [Unfinished Tales, “The Drúedain”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

## Second Age

Most of Beleriand has been drowned. Lindon occupies surviving western lands around the Gulf of Lhûn. Three distances shape the politics of the Age: the sea between Númenor and its mainland havens, the road between Lindon and Eregion, and the short passage through the mountains joining Eregion to the Anduin side. Dates below follow Appendix B unless a variant is indicated. [*The Lord of the Rings*, Appendix B, “The Second Age”](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp)

| Kingdom or region | Who lives or holds power there | Places and changes that matter in play |
|---|---|---|
| Lindon | Gil-galad’s kingdom. The Gulf of Lhûn separates Forlindon in the north from Harlindon in the south. | Mithlond, the Grey Havens, is Círdan’s charge. Warnings about Annatar can travel east from here. The same shore receives ships from Númenor. |
| Eriador | The broad country between the Blue and Misty Mountains contains Elven settlements, Dwarven routes, and communities of Men. Its peoples have different rulers and interests. | The Lhûn, Baranduin, and Gwathló orient west-to-east and coastal journeys. Arnor belongs only on maps of the final part of the Age. |
| Eregion | The Noldorin realm of the jewel-smiths, founded in S.A. 750. Celebrimbor and the Gwaith-i-Mírdain work at Ost-in-Edhil. | The road to the West-gate of Khazad-dûm serves both peoples. For the Annatar campaign, choose Galadriel and Celeborn’s position in Eregion. Sauron destroys the realm in 1697. |
| Khazad-dûm and the central Misty Mountains | Durin’s Folk hold the Dwarven kingdom. People from the Blue Mountains swell its population early in the Age. | The West-gate faces Eregion. The East-gate opens into Dimrill Dale. Mithril and the friendship between the neighbouring craftspeople give a player reasons to travel in either direction. |
| Imladris | Elrond establishes a refuge in eastern Eriador in S.A. 1697 during the fall of Eregion. | The valley of the Bruinen offers a destination for refugees. An adventure set during Annatar’s early visits to the smiths predates Rivendell. |
| Lórinand and the Anduin vales | A Silvan land east of the Misty Mountains. One account gives it its own king, Amdír. Galadriel’s residence and authority depend on the history selected for the campaign. | The Celebrant and Anduin tie the woodland to Dwarven and river traffic. Farther up the Anduin, early hobbit households are an optional campaign conjecture. |
| Greenwood the Great | Oropher rules a Silvan people east of Anduin. His people move north during the Age. | Amon Lanc is an early seat. By the Last Alliance Oropher dwells near the Emyn Duir. The date matters to anyone sent across the forest to find him. |
| Minhiriath, Enedwaith, and the Gwathló | Forest communities live on both sides of the river. Númenórean demand for ship timber brings settlement, clearance, and resistance. | Aldarion establishes Vinyalondë, later Lond Daer, at the river mouth. A shipwright and a family living upriver have good reason to give different accounts of the haven’s growth. |
| Númenor | The island kingdom of Elros and his successors. The later division between the King’s Men and the Faithful has consequences on both shores of the sea. | Armenelos is the royal city, Andúnië a western haven, Rómenna an eastern haven, and Meneltarma the sacred mountain. The Downfall in S.A. 3319 ends island-based play. |
| The lower Anduin and the White Mountains | Peoples already live along the river and in the mountains before Gondor is founded. Númenórean settlers increasingly shape the coast. | Pelargir is founded in S.A. 2350 and becomes a chief haven of the Faithful. Erech belongs to a later story, when Isildur receives the mountain king’s oath. |
| Fangorn and the gardens of the Entwives | Ents dwell in the great woods. The Entwives tend lands east of Anduin and share their knowledge with Men. | The gardens give the eastern bank a history before it becomes known as the Brown Lands. The fate of individual Entwives remains open for the campaign. |
| Mordor and its northern approaches | Sauron establishes his power here during the Age. The land is protected by the Ephel Dúath and Ered Lithui. | Orodruin, Barad-dûr, and, late in the Age, the battlefield of Dagorlad. A campaign amid the first building of the Dark Tower has different roads and defences from one set during its siege. |
| Harad and Umbar | Near Harad, the haven of Umbar, and Far Harad give access to different parts of the South. Umbar is fortified in S.A. 2280. | [Southern homelands](#harad-and-the-southern-lands) include the pilots of Umbar, a valley kingdom inland, and the river towns of Far Harad. |
| Khand | A land southeast of Mordor, adjoining the wider southern and eastern theatres. The Variags appear in accounts of the Third Age. | The [Khand entry](#harad-and-the-southern-lands) offers a highland route and a succession dispute for earlier play. |
| Rhûn and the eastern lands | The inland Sea of Rhûn and the Celduin give eastern journeys a geographic centre. Beyond the lake, several peoples can share a trade road and owe allegiance to different rulers. | [Eastern homelands](#rhûn-and-the-eastern-lands) include the river mouth, an inland kingdom, and a meeting place in the grazing country. |
| Arnor | Elendil’s northern kingdom in exile, founded in S.A. 3320. | Annúminas on Lake Nenuial is the royal seat. A settlement can be older than the kingdom whose messengers now claim authority over it. |
| Gondor | The southern kingdom in exile, founded in S.A. 3320 and ruled jointly by Isildur and Anárion under Elendil’s high kingship. | Osgiliath is the chief city. Minas Ithil and Minas Anor stand on opposite sides of Anduin. Pelargir already has nearly a thousand years of its own history. |

For Númenor’s geography and overseas ambitions, see “A Description of the Island of Númenor,” “Aldarion and Erendis,” and the *Akallabêth*. For the woodland realms, including their variant histories, see “The History of Galadriel and Celeborn” and the notes to “The Disaster of the Gladden Fields.” Tolkien’s account of the end of the Age joins the stories of Mordor, Lindon, and the kingdoms in exile. [*Unfinished Tales*](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/) · [*Akallabêth*](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1232/akallab%C3%AAth) · [“Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1233/of-the-rings-of-power-and-the-third-age)

**The people of Greenwood.** East of the Misty Mountains the Silvan Elves already had their own speech and woodland life. In the tradition followed here, Oropher came from ruined Doriath with a small Sindarin company. They learned the local language and took names in Silvan fashion. Oropher became king. He fell in the War of the Last Alliance, and his son Thranduil brought the survivors home. Thranduil’s later halls recall Menegroth. The people who built and used them had a history in Greenwood long before his family arrived. [Unfinished Tales, “The History of Galadriel and Celeborn,” including “The Sindarin Princes of the Silvan Elves”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

**The pass before the Dark Tower.** Shelob had taken shelter in the western mountains before Sauron settled in Mordor. She served herself. Her victims included Elves and Men, though their homes and the date of her arrival are unrecorded. About S.A. 1000 Sauron chose Mordor as his stronghold and began Barad-dûr. Orodruin supplied fire for sorcery and forging. About S.A. 1600 he completed the tower and made the One Ring. [The Two Towers, “Shelob’s Lair”](https://bookwise.io/j-r-r-tolkien/the-lord-of-the-rings-volume-2) · [The Lord of the Rings, Appendix B, “The Second Age”](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp) · [The Silmarillion, “Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1233)

## Rhûn and the eastern lands

Rhûn means *East* in Sindarin. The Celduin runs into the Sea of Rhûn on the familiar map. Tolkien also gives the East a history of its own. In one Second Age note, some eastern Orcs laugh at Sauron in his fair form. His emissaries are already at work among eastern Men, and he still has peoples to persuade or subdue. Khamûl, one of the Nine, is called the Shadow of the East. His mortal home and the circumstances in which he accepted a Ring are open campaign questions. [Sea of Rhûn and the Celduin](https://www.encyclopedia-of-arda.com/s/seaofrhun.php) · [“Note on the Delay of Gil-galad and the Númenóreans”](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Eastern_Orcs) · [*Unfinished Tales*, “The Hunt for the Ring”](https://ebookscape.com/middle-earth/unfinished-tales-j-r-r-tolkien/)

The following eastern and southern homelands are **proposed campaign material**. Their English place-names translate what residents call them. Their settlements and institutions can be changed independently.

| Eastern homeland | People, government, and a reason to go there |
|---|---|
| **The Lower River**, at the mouth of the Celduin | Fishing islands and pilot villages share the delta. Each spring the pilots sound the channels and cut new marks into a common sounding board. When a channel shifts, two villages can honestly disagree about where fishing rights begin. A local scout can read several seasons of marks. |
| **The Copper Hills**, a small kingdom east of the lake | The crown maintains the smelting hearths. Mining families own the galleries, and the town below elects the officer who weighs the copper. An enchanter can apprentice to an assayer here. When a deep gallery floods, the queen offers the fishers downstream a voice in court in exchange for their pumps and boatwrights. Her heir opposes the promise. |
| **The Meeting Pastures**, farther inland | Herding households from several river valleys gather here for foaling, marriages, and an annual hearing of claims. Hospitality lasts through the hearing even between feuding houses. A bard called as a witness must decide whether a verse in their oldest grazing agreement names a family, a stream, or both. |

**An eastern campaign.** A solo character can begin as the Lower River pilot sent to the flooded mine. The first decision is which families to evacuate while the road is passable. Years later, if the GM locates the giving of one of the Nine here, the offer can come to a ruler the player already knows. Leave the recipient’s identity open until that choice matters. For a First Age game, one possible beginning is the first meeting of the herders at the river. Who offered hospitality when two households arrived with a claim to the same pasture? Let the player help establish the custom their descendants will inherit.

## Harad and the southern lands

Umbar has an excellent harbour long before Númenor makes it a fortress in S.A. 2280. Farther south, the histories occasionally preserve names. In the late Second Age, Herumor and Fuinur are Númenórean lords who rise to power among the Haradrim. Tolkien leaves their seats and the extent of their rule unspecified. Near Harad and Far Harad are broad western geographic terms. Khand has its own entry southeast of Mordor. Its better-attested Third Age alliances leave room to choose its earlier politics. [Appendix B, “The Second Age”](https://theallfreenovel.com/130/33/the_return_of_the_king.amp) · [“Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age”](https://martijn.coppoolse.com/books/part/1233/of-the-rings-of-power-and-the-third-age) · [*The Return of the King*, “The Battle of the Pelennor Fields”](https://www.ebookscape.com/middle-earth/the-return-of-the-king-j-r-r-tolkien/)

| Southern homeland | People, government, and a reason to go there |
|---|---|
| **The havens of Umbar**, on the coast | In this version pilots on each headland maintain lookout posts and owe rescue to any crew that signals. The harbour assembly pays their widows. Fortification brings a second authority into the harbour. When the garrison closes the entrance during a storm, a player who grew up here may know the pilot waiting outside. |
| **The Fig Vale**, a kingdom of Near Harad | Villages share terraced orchards and elect the keepers who divide the spring water. The royal house holds its land on the same terms. A disputed marriage settlement turns on who pledged water that belonged to other households. The keeper’s oldest tally comes from the bride’s grandmother, who is still alive and willing to testify. |
| **The Three Estuaries**, on the coast of Far Harad | Three river towns share a boatbuilding ground where families put up visiting crews during the launching season. Each town keeps its own law. A master builder has promised the first vessel of a new design to two different towns. Both crews arrive to launch her on the same tide. A local player can know the builder, the head of each crew, and precisely what each promise meant. |
| **The winter pass of Khand**, inland | In this version a line of hill rulers keeps the pass open. Each successor must spend a winter in the pass villages before receiving the seals of office. The lawful heir has vanished. The village that sheltered her knows why she left and whom she feared. The next caravan carries the claimant who would inherit in her absence. |

**A southern campaign.** In the First Age, play the wreck that first obliges the headland families to answer a stranger’s signal. In the Second, return to their successors when ships from Númenor begin calling. A navigator from the island might earn a place in a pilot household. Centuries later her descendants might stand on opposite sides of the harbour closure. For the late Age, a GM could let Herumor or Fuinur gain allies by enforcing the old rescue obligation against a garrison commander. The pilots have a concrete reason to welcome him, and a claim on him if he ever breaks his word.
